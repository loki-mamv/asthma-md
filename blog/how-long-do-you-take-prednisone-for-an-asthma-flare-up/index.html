How Long Do You Take Prednisone for an Asthma Flare-Up?

October 8, 2026 7 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team

The short answer: Most prednisone courses for an asthma flare-up are short. The 2026 GINA strategy report states a usual course of 5 to 7 days for adults and adolescents, and 3 to 5 days for children. The older NHLBI guideline allows a wider range of 3 to 10 days. Both say a course this short can stop without a taper.

How many days do the guidelines state?

Two guidelines agree on a short course. They differ on the exact range.

  • GINA (2026): The report states that the usual prednisone or prednisolone dose for adults is 40 to 50 mg a day for 5 to 7 days. For children aged 6 to 11, it states 1 to 2 mg per kg a day, to a maximum of 40 mg a day, usually for 3 to 5 days.
  • NHLBI Expert Panel Report 3 (2007): The section on managing exacerbations states an outpatient "burst" of 40 to 60 mg a day for a total of 5 to 10 days in adults. For children, it states 1 to 2 mg per kg a day, to a maximum of 60 mg a day, for 3 to 10 days.

These figures describe what the guidelines state. They are not instructions for you. Your prescriber sets your dose and your number of days, and your label may differ.

The FDA label gives no asthma-specific course length. The prednisone tablet label on DailyMed says the initial dose "may vary from 5 mg to 60 mg of prednisone per day", depending on the disease.

A flare-up plan usually starts with inhalers. See our guide to an asthma attack action plan for the steps that come before steroid tablets.

Do you need to taper prednisone after a short course?

Usually not. GINA states that tapering is not needed if oral corticosteroids are taken for less than 2 weeks. It states that tapering is required after more than 2 weeks.

The NHLBI guideline says the same for courses of less than 1 week. For courses up to 10 days, it says there is "probably" no need to taper, especially if you also take an inhaled corticosteroid.

The evidence comes from a small double-blind trial. O'Driscoll and colleagues (Lancet, 1993) studied 35 adults admitted to hospital with acute asthma. All took prednisolone for 10 days. Then half tapered over a week and half took placebo tablets. Peak flow did not differ between the groups. All patients used inhaled steroids.

Long-term use is different. The DailyMed label says that after long-term therapy the drug should be "withdrawn gradually rather than abruptly". MedlinePlus warns not to stop prednisone without talking to your doctor. If your prescription includes a taper, follow it.

Do shorter courses or dexamethasone work as well?

The evidence says yes for most adults. GINA states that 5-day and 7-day courses in adults were as effective as 10-day and 14-day courses.

One of those trials is Jones and colleagues (Respiratory Medicine, 2002). It followed 44 adults admitted to hospital with acute asthma. All took prednisolone for 5 days. Then 24 took 5 more days and 20 took placebo. Morning peak flow did not differ through day 21. One patient in each group needed more oral steroids.

The trial was small, and every patient also used an inhaled corticosteroid. The authors made that a condition of their conclusion. Our comparison of a rescue inhaler and a controller inhaler explains the difference.

Dexamethasone is a longer-acting steroid, and it allows an even shorter course. GINA lists oral dexamethasone for a total of 1 to 2 days as an option for children. It cites a systematic review that found no difference in relapse rate against 3 to 5 days of prednisone or prednisolone. Children vomited much less with dexamethasone.

An earlier meta-analysis in Pediatrics (2014) pooled six emergency department trials in children and found the same result.

The adult evidence is mixed:

  • Kravitz and colleagues (2011) compared 2 days of dexamethasone with 5 days of prednisone in 200 adults. Relapse was similar (13% versus 11%). More of the dexamethasone group returned to normal activity within 3 days (90% versus 80%).
  • Rehrer and colleagues (2016) compared a single dexamethasone dose with 5 days of prednisone in 376 adults. Relapse within 14 days was 12.1% versus 9.8%. The single dose narrowly failed the trial's test for noninferiority.

GINA adds a limit. It states that oral dexamethasone should not continue beyond 2 days because of concerns about metabolic side effects.

How fast does prednisone work for asthma?

It does not work in minutes. GINA states that oral corticosteroids need at least 4 hours to produce a clinical improvement. Its medication table states that the main effects appear after 4 to 6 hours.

This is why a reliever inhaler still matters during a flare-up. GINA also states that oral steroids are as effective as intravenous ones.

GINA tells patients to contact their clinician if a flare-up does not respond to increased inhaled treatment over 2 to 3 days. Do not wait if your symptoms get worse on prednisone.

What side effects can a short course cause?

Most short-course effects are mild and stop after the course ends. GINA lists these common ones for adults:

  • Sleep disturbance
  • Increased appetite
  • Reflux
  • Mood changes
  • High blood sugar

GINA states that morning dosing is preferable to reduce insomnia. Flare-ups already disturb sleep, as we explain in why asthma is worse at night. For general sleep help, see insomnia.md.

The DailyMed label describes psychiatric effects that range from "euphoria, insomnia, mood swings" to more severe changes. It also lists "increased requirements for insulin or oral hypoglycemic agents in diabetics". People with diabetes should ask how to monitor glucose during a course.

Rare serious events also occur. A BMJ cohort study of about 1.5 million US adults (2017) looked at the 30 days after a short steroid prescription. Rates rose for sepsis (incidence rate ratio 5.30), blood clots (3.33) and fracture (1.87). These are relative rates, and the risk fell over the next 31 to 90 days. Most of these prescriptions were not for asthma.

How many prednisone courses a year are too many?

Fewer than most people expect. GINA lists "frequent courses of oral corticosteroids (e.g., two or more courses a year)" as a reason to consider referral for expert advice.

The harm adds up across courses. GINA states that in adults, even 4 to 5 lifetime courses are linked to a dose-dependent rise in the risk of diabetes, cataract, heart failure and osteoporosis. That statement rests on a matched cohort study of 24,117 pairs of adults with asthma.

A US claims analysis (2018) found that adults with 4 or more oral steroid prescriptions in a year had 1.29 times the odds of a new adverse effect that year. Both studies are observational, so they cannot prove the cause.

GINA states that any flare-up that needs oral steroids should prompt a review of your asthma treatment. For people with severe asthma, GINA states that biologic therapy substantially reduces the need for oral steroids.

When does a flare-up need emergency care?

Prednisone at home is not a substitute for emergency care. The NHLBI page on asthma attacks gives these signs:

  • Call 9-1-1 if your medicines do not relieve your symptoms, or if breathing is still very hard.
  • Go to the emergency room if symptoms do not go away soon after your at-home medicines.
  • For a young child, call 9-1-1 if the child seems drowsy or confused, has bluish skin or lips, or has a fast heartbeat.

NHLBI also lists a recent need for oral corticosteroids as a reason to seek care right away during an attack.

The bottom line

GINA states a prednisone course of 5 to 7 days for adults and 3 to 5 days for children. NHLBI allows 3 to 10 days. Courses this short do not need a taper. Two or more courses in a year is a signal to review your asthma treatment with your clinician.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I stop prednisone early if my asthma feels better?

Talk to your prescriber first. MedlinePlus warns not to stop prednisone without talking to your doctor. GINA states a usual course of 5 to 7 days for adults, and the trials behind that figure tested full courses.

What time of day should I take prednisone for asthma?

GINA states that after the first dose, morning dosing is preferable to reduce insomnia. MedlinePlus says prednisone is usually taken with food. Follow the timing on your own prescription label.

Is prednisolone the same as prednisone?

They are closely related oral corticosteroids. GINA and NHLBI give the same dose and course length for both in asthma flare-ups. Children often get prednisolone because it comes as a liquid, and GINA states that a liquid form is preferred for children.

Does a short prednisone course raise blood sugar?

It can. GINA lists high blood sugar among the short-term effects of short courses. The FDA label lists increased insulin or oral diabetes medicine requirements in people with diabetes, so ask your clinician how to monitor glucose during a course.

Should I keep using my inhalers while I take prednisone?

Yes, unless your clinician says otherwise. Prednisone needs at least 4 hours to produce improvement, according to GINA, so your reliever still handles quick relief. The trials that supported short courses were done in people who also used inhaled corticosteroids.
