What Should You Do During an Asthma Attack if You Don't Have Your Inhaler?

The short answer: If you are having an asthma attack and have no inhaler, call 911. The NHS lists "do not have an asthma inhaler" as a reason to call for an ambulance on its own. While you wait, sit upright, move away from the trigger, and try to breathe slowly. Coffee, steam, and breathing tricks cannot replace rescue medicine during a real attack.

When should you call 911 if you have no inhaler?

Most attack advice assumes you can take quick-relief puffs first and then judge whether they worked. Without an inhaler, you lose that first step and the safety check that comes with it. That is why the threshold for calling for help is lower.

The National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute (NHLBI) says attacks "can come on quickly or gradually and may be life-threatening." It advises calling 911 if medicines are not relieving symptoms or breathing is still very hard. With no medicine at all, you are already in that situation once symptoms are more than mild.

The CDC asthma action plan lists danger signs that mean you need help right away:

  • Trouble walking or talking because of shortness of breath
  • Lips or fingernails that look blue, pale, or gray

For young children, NHLBI adds three more signs that call for 911: seeming drowsy, confused, or troubled; a blue tint to the skin and lips; and a fast heartbeat. If you see any of these, do not wait to see if things settle.

The NHS also tells people not to drive themselves to the emergency room during an attack. The dispatcher can give you instructions while help is on the way.

What can you do while you wait for help?

None of these steps opens narrowed airways the way a bronchodilator does. They can help you stay as steady as possible until care arrives.

  1. Sit up straight. The NHS advice is to "sit up straight, to help your breathing."
  2. Get away from the trigger. Smoke, strong fumes, cold air, dust, or an animal can keep driving the attack. Move to cleaner air if you can do it safely. Our guide to common asthma triggers lists the usual suspects.
  3. Try to stay calm. The NHS includes this step too. Panic speeds up breathing, which can make the tight, air-hungry feeling worse.
  4. Breathe slowly. Slow, steady breaths may help you feel more in control. Do not force deep breaths.
  5. Loosen tight clothing around your chest and neck, and ask someone to stay with you.
  6. Ask for a nearby inhaler of your own. Check your car, bag, desk, or a family member's home for a spare that was prescribed to you.

If your symptoms ease, you still need medical follow-up. The NHS advises seeing a doctor within 2 days after any asthma attack, even after you recover.

Does coffee or caffeine help during an asthma attack?

Caffeine is a weak bronchodilator. It is chemically related to theophylline, an older asthma drug. That is where the coffee tip comes from.

A 2010 Cochrane review pooled seven small trials with 75 adults who had mild to moderate asthma. Caffeine improved lung function modestly for up to 2 to 4 hours. On average, FEV1 (the amount of air you can blow out in 1 second) rose by about 5%.

Three limits matter here:

  • The trials tested people with stable asthma, not people in the middle of an attack.
  • A 5% gain is small next to what a rescue inhaler is designed to do.
  • The review's main practical message was for lung testing: people may need to avoid caffeine for at least 4 hours before a lung function test, because it can skew results.

So a cup of coffee is not a treatment for an attack. It also takes time to absorb, and drinking can be hard when you are short of breath. Do not let it delay a 911 call.

Do steam, breathing exercises, or other home remedies work?

Steam, hot showers, and "breathe into a paper bag" are common internet tips. None of the NHLBI, CDC, or NHS guidance we reviewed lists steam or home remedies as treatment for an attack. All three point to reliever medicine and emergency care.

Breathing exercises do have some evidence, but for day-to-day control. A 2020 Cochrane review of 22 trials with 2,880 adults found that methods like yoga breathing, Buteyko, and the Papworth method improved quality of life at 3 months. Those studies enrolled outpatients with mild to moderate asthma. They did not test whether breathing exercises can stop an acute attack.

Some people mistake a panic attack for an asthma attack, or have both at once. If anxiety often drives your symptoms, anxiety.md covers how to tell them apart. But when you have known asthma and cannot breathe, treat it as asthma first.

Can you use someone else's inhaler or buy one over the counter?

In the US, albuterol rescue inhalers need a prescription. The FDA label for Ventolin HFA calls it "a prescription inhaled medicine." MedlinePlus says to follow the directions on your prescription label and use albuterol exactly as directed.

The guidance we reviewed does not tell people to use someone else's inhaler. This article does not recommend it either. Prescription inhalers are chosen and dosed for one person. If you are in trouble and without your own medicine, the safe move is to call 911 and follow the dispatcher's advice.

There is one over-the-counter option. The FDA approved Primatene Mist, an epinephrine inhaler, on November 7, 2018. Its approved use is narrow: "temporary relief of mild symptoms of intermittent asthma" in people 12 and older.

The Primatene Mist label carries clear limits:

  • Do not use it unless a doctor has said you have asthma.
  • See a doctor if you are not better in 20 minutes or you get worse.
  • It can raise blood pressure and heart rate, which can raise the risk of heart attack or stroke.
  • Ask a doctor first if you have heart disease, high blood pressure, diabetes, thyroid disease, or seizures.
  • Avoid caffeine while using it.

It is not labeled for severe attacks and does not replace an emergency call.

How can you avoid being caught without an inhaler again?

Most no-inhaler emergencies are preventable with a bit of planning:

  • Ask for more than one reliever. Many people keep one at home, one in a bag, and one at work or school. Ask your clinician and pharmacist what your coverage allows.
  • Track how full it is. Each albuterol aerosol inhaler gives 60 or 200 puffs, per MedlinePlus. After that, later puffs may not deliver the right dose. See how to know when your inhaler is empty.
  • Have a written action plan. NHLBI says your plan should explain how to tell if asthma is getting worse and when to get emergency care. Our asthma attack action plan guide walks through the zones.
  • Treat frequent reliever use as a warning. NHLBI flags using more than one canister of reliever a month as a sign of higher risk. That calls for a review of your daily treatment.
  • Know your controller. If you are on a daily steroid inhaler, keep taking it. Our page on rescue versus controller inhalers explains which one does what.

The bottom line

An asthma attack with no inhaler is a reason to call 911, not to wait it out. Sit upright, leave the trigger, stay as calm as you can, and breathe slowly while help comes. Caffeine has only a small, slow effect in stable asthma, and steam or breathing tricks are not attack treatments. Albuterol needs a prescription in the US, and the only over-the-counter inhaler is labeled for mild symptoms only. The best fix is prevention: spare inhalers, a full canister, and a written action plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you stop an asthma attack without an inhaler?

Mild symptoms sometimes ease once you leave the trigger and rest upright. But there is no proven home method that reliably stops an attack. If breathing is hard or getting worse and you have no inhaler, call 911.

How long can an asthma attack last without treatment?

There is no safe time to wait. NHLBI says attacks can come on quickly or gradually and may be life-threatening. The NHS advises calling for an ambulance if you are having an attack and do not have an inhaler.

Will an ER give me albuterol if I don't have my inhaler?

Emergency teams treat asthma attacks with medicines such as nebulized bronchodilators, steroids, and oxygen when needed. NHLBI notes that emergency care may include a nebulizer, an IV line, and in severe cases a ventilator. Ask for a new prescription and follow-up before you leave.

Is Primatene Mist the same as albuterol?

No. Primatene Mist contains epinephrine, while rescue inhalers like Ventolin HFA contain albuterol. Primatene Mist is sold over the counter for temporary relief of mild intermittent asthma symptoms in people 12 and older. Albuterol inhalers need a prescription in the US.

Should kids with asthma keep an inhaler at school?

Most action plans assume the reliever is close at hand, and an inhaler locked at home cannot help during an attack at school. Ask your child's clinician to fill out a school action plan and ask the school how it stores student medicines. Many families keep a separate inhaler just for school.
