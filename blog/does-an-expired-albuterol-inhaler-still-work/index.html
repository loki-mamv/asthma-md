Does an Expired Albuterol Inhaler Still Work?

October 15, 2026 7 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: An expired albuterol inhaler probably still releases some medicine, but nobody can promise it releases the full dose. A 2022 lab study found expired inhalers held 73% to 103% of their labeled strength. The FDA label says to throw the inhaler away at the expiration date or when the counter reaches zero, whichever comes first. If an expired inhaler is all you have during an attack, using it is better than nothing, but replace it right away.

What does the expiration date on an inhaler mean?

The date is a promise from the maker, backed by testing. Federal rules require every drug to carry an expiration date set by stability testing. The goal, in the words of 21 CFR 211.137, is to make sure the product "meets applicable standards of identity, strength, quality, and purity at the time of use."

That promise has a condition. The same rule ties the date to the storage conditions on the label. An inhaler kept in a hot car is not covered by the date in the same way as one kept in a drawer.

The date does not mean the drug turns harmful the next day. It marks the end of the period the maker has tested and will stand behind. The FDA's consumer guidance puts it plainly: "Once the expiration date has passed there is no guarantee that the medicine will be safe and effective." The FDA advises against using expired medicine. Expiration dates have been required on prescription and over-the-counter drugs since 1979.

Has anyone tested expired albuterol inhalers?

Yes, but only in a small study. Researchers at the Medical College of Wisconsin tested donated medicines from a free clinic. They published the results in Heliyon in 2022, after a short report in Annals of Allergy, Asthma and Immunology. The samples had sat at room temperature in the clinic, sealed and unopened unless noted.

They measured albuterol with high performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), a standard lab method. Here is what they found:

  • Nebulizer solutions: 11 expired batches, with expiration dates from 2001 to 2019. Most held more than 90% of their labeled strength. Two older lots held 85% to 88%.
  • Inhalers: 5 expired Ventolin and Proventil batches, expired from 2009 to 2017. Measured strength ranged from 73% to 103%.
  • Breakdown products: no major degradation products showed up in any sample.

The inhaler results need a caveat. The team sprayed puffs into a balloon and measured what they caught. Some spray was likely lost. An in-date Ventolin inhaler tested the same way read only 87%. When the authors compared expired inhalers to that in-date one, the GSK inhalers read above 100%. The Proventil lots read about 84% to 95%.

The study has clear limits. It tested only 5 expired inhalers. It measured drug content, not how well people breathed after using them. It did not test inhalers left in hot cars or bags. It is useful evidence that albuterol is a stable molecule, not proof that your own old inhaler works.

Why do many drugs outlast their printed date?

Makers set dates based on the testing they have done, not on the day a drug fails. The best data on this comes from the Shelf Life Extension Program, which the FDA ran for the U.S. Department of Defense. An FDA review of that program covered 122 drug products and 3,005 lots.

Of those lots, 88% were extended at least 1 year past the original date, with an average extension of 66 months. But the review stressed that results varied from lot to lot. Its authors wrote that quality "can only be assured by periodic testing and systematic evaluation of each lot." Those drugs were stored under controlled conditions. A home inhaler gets no such testing.

Can an inhaler run out before it expires?

Yes, and this is the more common problem. A canister holds a fixed number of sprays. The Ventolin HFA label lists 200-spray and 60-spray sizes. If you use it often, it can empty long before the date on the box.

The dose counter is the guide. The Ventolin HFA counter starts at 204 or 064, because priming uses 4 sprays. The label says to refill when it reads 020 and throw the inhaler away at 000. It warns that past 000 the right dose "cannot be assured," even though the canister "will continue to operate."

The ProAir HFA label says the same thing in one line. The inhaler "should be discarded when the dose counter displays 0 or after the expiration date on the product, whichever comes first." For more on reading counters, see our guide on how to know when an inhaler is empty. If you go through canisters fast, read how many times a day you can use an albuterol inhaler. Frequent use is a sign your asthma needs review.

Priming matters too. The Ventolin HFA label says to prime with 4 sprays before first use, after 2 weeks without use, or after a drop. A rescue inhaler that sits unused for months needs priming before you rely on it.

How do heat and cold affect an albuterol inhaler?

Storage can matter as much as the date. The Ventolin HFA label says to store it between 68 and 77 degrees F (20 to 25 degrees C), with brief swings allowed from 59 to 86 degrees F. It says to store it mouthpiece down and to let it reach room temperature before use. The ProAir HFA label says to avoid extreme heat and cold.

Heat is also a safety issue. Both labels warn that temperatures above 120 degrees F may cause the canister to burst. A car in summer can pass that. MedlinePlus adds that inhalers should be kept away from excess heat and moisture, and not in the bathroom. The FDA notes that a damp bathroom cabinet can reduce a medicine's effect.

So an inhaler that is a few months past its date but kept in a cool drawer is a different case from one that spent a summer in a glove box. The lab study above only tested inhalers kept at room temperature.

What should you do with an expired inhaler?

Replace it. This is a rescue drug, and a weak dose during an attack is a real risk. Some practical steps:

  1. Check dates now. Look at every inhaler you keep at home, at work, in a car, or in a school bag. The date is on the box and the canister.
  2. Ask for a refill early. Do not wait until the counter hits zero or the date passes.
  3. In an emergency, use what you have. If an expired inhaler is the only one on hand, the lab data suggest it likely still delivers some medicine. Use it while you get help, then replace it.
  4. Get help if it does not work. If symptoms do not improve, follow your action plan and call 911. Our asthma attack action plan covers the warning signs.
  5. Dispose of it safely. The canister is under pressure. Do not puncture it or throw it into a fire or incinerator. MedlinePlus suggests a medicine take-back program, and your pharmacist can tell you about local options.

Running low on inhalers often happens alongside a rough asthma stretch, and that can bring on worry about the next attack. If breathless spells come with racing thoughts and fear, anxiety.md covers how panic and breathing problems overlap.

The bottom line

In the one lab study of expired albuterol, no major breakdown products appeared, and tests suggest it keeps much of its strength for years. But the only inhaler study tested 5 canisters, and results ran as low as 73%. The FDA label sets a simple rule: discard at the expiration date or when the counter reads zero, whichever comes first. Keep a current rescue inhaler where you need it, store it at room temperature, and use an expired one only as a stopgap.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it dangerous to use an albuterol inhaler a month after it expires?

There is no evidence that expired albuterol becomes toxic. The main risk is a smaller dose than you expect. Lab testing of expired inhalers found 73% to 103% of labeled strength. Replace it as soon as you can.

Does an expired inhaler keep spraying normally?

Usually yes, because the propellant keeps working. A normal-looking spray does not prove a full dose of medicine. The labels warn that a canister can keep spraying even when the right dose cannot be assured.

Can I keep a spare albuterol inhaler in my car?

The labels say to store albuterol inhalers at room temperature and warn that temperatures above 120 degrees F may cause the canister to burst. Cars can get hotter than that in summer. A bag you carry with you is a safer place than a glove box.

Will my pharmacy or insurance cover a replacement for an expired inhaler?

Coverage depends on your plan and how often it allows refills. Ask your pharmacist, who can check your plan and contact your prescriber if needed. Do not wait until an attack to find out.

How should I throw away an old albuterol inhaler?

Do not puncture the canister or put it in a fire, because it is under pressure. MedlinePlus recommends a medicine take-back program. Your pharmacist can tell you where to find one near you.
