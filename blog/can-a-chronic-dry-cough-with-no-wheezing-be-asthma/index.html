Can a Chronic Dry Cough With No Wheezing Be Asthma?

October 6, 2026 6 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team

The short answer: Yes. Cough-variant asthma is a form of asthma in which a persistent cough is the only symptom. There is no wheeze, and a routine breathing test is usually normal. A 2025 review estimates that it accounts for 25% to 42% of chronic cough cases. A doctor confirms it with a bronchial challenge test or with a cough that clears on asthma treatment.

What is cough-variant asthma?

Most people think of asthma as wheezing and shortness of breath. Cough-variant asthma has neither. The GINA 2026 strategy report describes it this way: a persistent cough is the only reported symptom. GINA uses a second term, cough-predominant asthma, when cough is the most prominent symptom but not the only one.

The cough is often dry, but not always. GINA says it may be worse at night or with exercise, and that in some patients it brings up mucus.

The airway changes are the same kind as in classic asthma. GINA says most patients with cough-variant asthma have eosinophils (a type of white blood cell) in their sputum. The airways are also twitchy, which doctors call airway hyperresponsiveness.

How common is asthma among chronic cough causes?

First, the definition. A cough is chronic in an adult when it lasts longer than 8 weeks. The 2018 CHEST cough guideline and the 2020 European Respiratory Society (ERS) guideline both use that cut-off. The ERS guideline uses 4 weeks for children.

The CHEST panel reviewed 11 studies of adults with chronic cough from four continents. The most common causes were:

  • Upper airway cough syndrome from nose and sinus conditions (once called postnasal drip syndrome)
  • Asthma
  • Gastroesophageal reflux disease
  • Non-asthmatic eosinophilic bronchitis
  • Combinations of these four

The panel rated the quality of that evidence as low. The share due to cough-variant asthma differs from clinic to clinic. The 2025 review gives a range of 25% to 42% and says the condition is likely underdiagnosed. The ERS guideline says about a quarter of chronic cough patients have the type 2 airway inflammation seen in cough-variant asthma and eosinophilic bronchitis.

How do doctors tell it apart from other causes of a dry cough?

The cough itself does not give the answer. GINA lists the conditions below among the common causes of an isolated dry cough. Two of them can occur together.

Upper airway cough syndrome. Allergic rhinitis, non-allergic rhinitis, and sinusitis are typical causes. The ACCP guideline on this syndrome says its symptoms and signs are nonspecific. History and examination alone cannot make a definite diagnosis. A person with none of the usual signs may still respond to treatment for it.

Reflux. The 2016 CHEST guideline on reflux cough found that proton pump inhibitors showed no benefit for cough when used alone. It says diet changes and weight loss may help. The ERS guideline reports a possible modest effect of these medicines in people who have acid reflux. See can acid reflux make asthma worse for the asthma link.

ACE inhibitor cough. ACE inhibitors are common blood pressure medicines. The ACCP guideline on this cough reports cough in 5% to 35% of people who take them. It can start within hours of the first dose or months later. It usually clears 1 to 4 weeks after the medicine is stopped, but may last up to 3 months. Do not stop the medicine on your own. Your prescriber can change it. hypertension.md covers blood pressure medicines.

Non-asthmatic eosinophilic bronchitis. This is the closest look-alike. The ACCP guideline on eosinophilic bronchitis says the airway inflammation is similar to asthma. The difference is that there is no variable airflow limitation and no airway hyperresponsiveness. A bronchial challenge test is therefore normal. The ERS guideline reports this condition in up to 13% of patients at cough clinics.

How is cough-variant asthma diagnosed?

The ERS guideline says the first evaluation of a chronic cough includes spirometry and a recent chest X-ray. In cough-variant asthma, spirometry is usually normal. Our article on asthma with normal spirometry explains why a normal result does not rule asthma out.

The next step is often a bronchial challenge. GINA says airway hyperresponsiveness on this test may be the only lung function abnormality. It warns that asthma may be missed in people with chronic cough if challenge testing is not done. See what happens during a methacholine challenge test.

A positive challenge is not the last word. The ACCP guideline on cough due to asthma says the diagnosis is confirmed only when the cough resolves with asthma treatment.

Some clinics measure FeNO (exhaled nitric oxide) or blood eosinophils. GINA says FeNO may be elevated in cough-variant asthma. The 2020 CHEST guideline on cough due to asthma rated the evidence for these biomarkers in chronic cough as weak.

How is cough-variant asthma treated?

GINA says treatment follows the usual recommendations for asthma, and that treatment containing an inhaled corticosteroid is effective. The 2020 CHEST guideline agrees on the order. Inhaled corticosteroids are the best option for cough due to asthma. A leukotriene receptor antagonist comes next.

Two more points come from the ERS guideline:

  • It suggests a short trial of an inhaled corticosteroid, 2 to 4 weeks, in adults with chronic cough.
  • It does not recommend a bronchodilator alone as maintenance treatment for asthmatic cough.

The ACCP guideline adds that some patients need a short course of oral corticosteroids. Your clinician chooses the medicine and the dose.

Does cough-variant asthma turn into classic asthma?

It can. GINA says some patients later develop wheeze and a positive bronchodilator test. The numbers we found come from studies of children.

  • In a 4-year Korean study, 24 of 51 children (47.1%) developed signs of classic asthma.
  • In a Japanese study with at least 3 years of follow-up, classic asthma developed in 54% of children who responded.

Early treatment may lower the risk. In a randomized trial of 84 children aged 2 to 6 years, wheezing developed in 7.1% of the inhaled corticosteroid group over 24 months. The rate was 33.3% in the montelukast group. This was one small trial, so the result is not certain.

When does a chronic cough need urgent care?

Some signs need fast attention. MedlinePlus says to call 911 or your local emergency number for shortness of breath or difficulty breathing. It also lists hives or a swollen face or throat with difficulty swallowing.

MedlinePlus says to contact your provider right away for a cough with any of these:

  • Blood in what you cough up
  • Unintentional weight loss or night sweats
  • Fever
  • Swelling in your legs, or a cough that gets worse when you lie down
  • A high-pitched sound when you breathe in
  • Contact with someone who has tuberculosis

A cough with other symptoms you cannot explain also deserves a visit. symptom.md covers common symptom patterns.

The bottom line

A dry cough that lasts longer than 8 weeks can be asthma, even with no wheeze and a normal breathing test. Cough-variant asthma is one of the common causes of chronic cough. Nose and sinus conditions, reflux, ACE inhibitors, and eosinophilic bronchitis cause the same symptom, so testing matters. A bronchial challenge test and a response to inhaled corticosteroid treatment confirm the diagnosis. Treatment follows usual asthma care. See a clinician soon if you cough up blood, lose weight, or become short of breath.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a cough have to last to count as chronic?

In adults, longer than 8 weeks. The 2018 CHEST cough guideline calls a cough acute under 3 weeks and subacute from 3 to 8 weeks. The European Respiratory Society guideline uses 4 weeks as the cut-off for children.

Can cough-variant asthma cause a wet cough?

Yes, in some people. The cough is usually dry, but the GINA 2026 report says it is productive in some patients. GINA also says the cough may be worse at night or with exercise.

Will a rescue inhaler alone treat cough-variant asthma?

Guidelines do not support that. The European Respiratory Society guideline does not recommend a bronchodilator alone as maintenance treatment for asthmatic cough. GINA says treatment follows usual asthma recommendations and that treatment containing an inhaled corticosteroid is effective.

Do I need a CT scan for a chronic cough?

Usually not as a routine step. The European Respiratory Society guideline suggests that clinicians do not routinely order a chest CT when the chest X-ray and physical examination are normal. Your clinician may order one if other findings point to lung disease.

Can children have cough-variant asthma?

Yes. GINA says cough may be the only symptom of asthma in some children and adults. In children, a dry cough at night may be the only symptom a parent notices. Questions may show that the cough also comes with exercise, crying, or laughing.
