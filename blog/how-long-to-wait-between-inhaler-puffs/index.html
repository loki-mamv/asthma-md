How Long Should You Wait Between Puffs of an Inhaler?

September 20, 2026 7 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: The interval depends on the device, and the printed Instructions for Use are the only authority. Most albuterol rescue inhalers, including ProAir HFA and Ventolin HFA, tell you to wait 1 minute between sprays and shake the inhaler again. Other inhalers give a different number or none at all. Flovent HFA says about 30 seconds and Atrovent HFA says at least 15 seconds.

What do albuterol inhaler labels say about the wait?

The FDA label for an inhaler has two parts that matter here. The Dosage and Administration section tells the prescriber how much and how often. The Instructions for Use tells the patient how to work the device. The wait between puffs lives in the second part, not the first.

The ProAir HFA prescribing information sets the dose at two inhalations repeated every 4 to 6 hours for adults and children 4 years and older. It adds that more frequent administration or a larger number of inhalations is not recommended. That section says nothing about an interval. The patient instructions later in the same document do: "If your doctor has told you to use more sprays, wait 1 minute and shake the inhaler again."

Other short-acting bronchodilators say close to the same thing. Ventolin HFA uses the identical sentence. Proventil HFA says to wait 1 minute, shake the inhaler again, and repeat the steps. Xopenex HFA, which contains levalbuterol, says to wait about 1 minute and then shake well. So the one minute figure is not folklore. It is printed in FDA-approved labeling for several rescue inhalers. For how many doses per day the same labels allow, see our guide to how often you can use an albuterol inhaler.

Is the five minute rule between puffs real?

We read the current labels for ProAir HFA, Ventolin HFA, Proventil HFA, Xopenex HFA, Atrovent HFA, Flovent HFA, Symbicort, Advair Diskus, Spiriva Respimat and Qvar RediHaler. None of them states a five minute wait between the puffs of a single dose.

The official range is much shorter. Atrovent HFA tells patients to wait at least 15 seconds before repeating the steps. Flovent HFA says to wait about 30 seconds. The National Library of Medicine patient pages on using an inhaler without a spacer and with a spacer both say to wait 1 to 2 minutes before the next puff of a quick-relief medicine.

The longer waits people remember usually come from a different situation: repeat treatments during an attack, not the two puffs of one routine dose. In a 2026 randomized trial in Finnish pediatric emergency departments, children received salbutamol in cycles 20 minutes apart (JAMA Pediatrics). That is a clinician-supervised schedule, not a home instruction for the two puffs of one dose.

Does the wait differ for a steroid or combination inhaler?

Yes. Flovent HFA, an inhaled corticosteroid in a metered-dose inhaler, tells patients to wait about 30 seconds and shake the inhaler well for 5 seconds before the second puff. That is half the interval printed for albuterol.

Symbicort, a budesonide and formoterol combination inhaler, is dosed as 2 inhalations twice daily. Its Instructions for Use give no wait at all. Step 10 simply says to shake the inhaler again for 5 seconds and repeat the previous steps. The shaking is the required action, not the pause.

Both steroid labels add a step that rescue inhalers do not. Flovent HFA says to rinse your mouth with water after the medicine, spit it out, and not swallow. Symbicort says to rinse after both puffs. That step lowers the risk of oral thrush. A white coating, soreness or a hoarse voice are trackable symptoms worth raising with your clinician. Our comparison of a rescue inhaler and a controller inhaler explains why the two are used differently.

What about dry powder and soft mist inhalers?

For many of these devices the question does not arise, because a dose is one inhalation.

  • Dry powder inhaler. Advair Diskus is dosed as 1 inhalation twice daily for asthma in patients 4 years and older. There is no second puff to space out, no shaking and no priming.
  • Soft mist inhaler. Spiriva Respimat is given as 2 inhalations once daily, and the label states no interval between them. Combivent Respimat is 1 inhalation four times a day, not to exceed six inhalations in 24 hours.
  • Breath-actuated aerosol. Qvar RediHaler requires no priming, states that shaking before use is not necessary, and instructs that it must not be used with a spacer or volume holding chamber.

The lesson is that device type drives the steps. Our comparison of asthma inhalers covers how the categories differ.

What does a spacer change?

A spacer, or valved holding chamber, changes what you do between actuation and breath, more than what you do between puffs. The National Library of Medicine instructions for using an inhaler with a spacer say to spray one puff into the spacer at a time, and to wait 1 to 2 minutes before the next puff of a quick-relief medicine.

One puff at a time matters. A 2025 review of spacers and valved holding chambers lists the factors that change the dose leaving the chamber: chamber volume, shape, valve design, using multiple actuations, the delay between actuation and inhalation, and static charge on the chamber walls (Journal of Aerosol Medicine and Pulmonary Drug Delivery).

A chamber also protects against mistimed breathing. In a 2025 laboratory study, starting the inhalation at the moment of actuation, rather than before it, cut the delivered dose of fluticasone propionate and ciclesonide sharply. Adding a valved holding chamber restored much of that dose (Journal of Asthma and Allergy).

Chamber choice can change results in real patients. In the Finnish trial of 80 children aged 6 to 48 months with acute wheezing, the group using the higher-delivery chamber had a mean post-treatment respiratory distress score of 2.7, against 6.8 for the lower-delivery chamber, a mean difference of -4.1 (95% CI -5.4 to -2.7) (JAMA Pediatrics). For the youngest children, note that the Ventolin HFA label says safety and effectiveness with or without a spacer have not been demonstrated under 4 years of age.

Should you wait between two different inhalers?

A common teaching says to take the bronchodilator first, wait, then take the steroid, on the theory that open airways carry the steroid deeper. None of the labels we read instructs that. The ProAir HFA, Ventolin HFA, Flovent HFA, Symbicort and Advair Diskus labels set no interval between one product and the next, and we found no controlled trial showing that the order or the pause improves asthma outcomes.

The steps the labels do insist on are mechanical, and they are easy to skip:

  1. Priming. ProAir HFA is primed with 3 sprays into the air before first use, or after more than 2 weeks unused. Ventolin HFA and Flovent HFA use 4 sprays. Symbicort uses 2 test sprays, and is re-primed after 7 days unused or after being dropped.
  2. Shaking. Aerosol labels say to shake well before each spray. The NLM patient page says to shake the inhaler hard 10 to 15 times before each use.
  3. Breath hold. The albuterol labels say to hold your breath for about 10 seconds, or as long as is comfortable, then breathe out slowly.
  4. Rinsing. Steroid-containing inhalers say to rinse the mouth with water and spit it out.

Those four steps decide how much drug reaches your airways. The stopwatch between puffs is the smallest part of the technique.

The bottom line

There is no single interval for all inhalers. Read the Instructions for Use leaflet that came with your own device, because that is the number the FDA approved for that product. For most albuterol rescue inhalers it is 1 minute with a shake in between. For Flovent HFA it is about 30 seconds. For Atrovent HFA it is at least 15 seconds. For Symbicort, Advair Diskus and the Respimat inhalers, the label gives no interval at all. If your leaflet is lost, your pharmacist can print it, and the same text is public on DailyMed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does waiting longer between puffs make the second puff work better?

The labels do not say so. They set a short interval, from 15 seconds to about 1 minute depending on the product, and pair it with shaking the inhaler again. None of the labels we read promises extra benefit from a longer pause.

What happens if I take both puffs back to back without waiting?

The labels do not describe an outcome for that, so we cannot state one. What they do require is shaking the canister again before the second spray, because the medicine and propellant separate between uses. Ask your pharmacist to watch your technique if you are unsure.

Do I need to shake every inhaler before each spray?

No. Aerosol inhalers such as ProAir HFA, Ventolin HFA, Flovent HFA and Symbicort all say to shake before each spray. Qvar RediHaler states that shaking is not necessary. Advair Diskus is a dry powder device and is not shaken.

When should I rinse my mouth after a steroid inhaler?

Flovent HFA says to rinse with water after breathing in the medicine, spit the water out, and not swallow it. Symbicort says to rinse after you finish both puffs. The step reduces the chance of a yeast infection in the mouth and throat.

Does the interval change when a young child uses a mask and spacer?

The NLM spacer instructions say to spray one puff into the chamber at a time and to wait 1 to 2 minutes before the next puff of a quick-relief medicine. For children under 4 years, the Ventolin HFA label says safety and effectiveness with or without a spacer have not been demonstrated. Follow your pediatric clinician's written plan.
