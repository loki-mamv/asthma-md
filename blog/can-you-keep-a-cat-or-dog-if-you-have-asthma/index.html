Can You Keep a Cat or Dog If You Have Asthma?

October 11, 2026 7 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: Often, yes. A pet is a problem for your asthma only if you are allergic to it and it makes your symptoms worse. If that is true for you, removing the pet from the home is the most direct fix, but the 2020 NIH asthma guideline update calls the few studies on pet removal inconclusive. The decision is yours to make with your clinician, and it starts with an allergy test.

Does every person with asthma react to pets?

No. Pets affect asthma through allergy. If you are not allergic to your animal, it is unlikely to be a trigger for you.

The allergy is not to fur. The National Institute of Environmental Health Sciences (NIEHS) explains that fur only carries the allergens. Cat allergens come from saliva and from glands in the skin. Dog allergens are in dander, saliva, urine, and blood.

The NIH guideline reflects this. For people with asthma who have no allergy symptoms and no positive test for an indoor allergen such as cat dander, the expert panel recommends no specific steps to reduce that allergen at home. See how allergies and asthma connect.

How do you find out if your pet is the trigger?

Start with the pattern of your symptoms. Then confirm it with a test. The guideline says the trigger should be "confirmed by history taking or allergy testing." The two usual tests are a skin prick test and a blood test for allergen-specific IgE antibodies.

Signs that point to a pet allergy:

  • You cough, wheeze, or feel chest tightness soon after you touch or hold the animal.
  • Your nose runs, you sneeze, or your eyes itch around the animal.
  • Your symptoms get better when you are away from home for a week or more.

Sneezing and itchy eyes have many causes. Our sibling site symptom.md covers those symptoms in more detail.

The NIH panel says testing "may be particularly worthwhile for those with chronic or uncontrolled symptoms."

Which steps to reduce pet allergen have evidence behind them?

The evidence is weaker than most people expect. The NIH clinician's guide to the 2020 update states that the studies reviewed "provided little evidence that allergen mitigation strategies are beneficial for improving asthma outcomes." Its main advice is to combine measures, because one measure alone often does not improve asthma.

A trial shows the problem. In a placebo-controlled trial, 35 cat-allergic adults who lived with cats got an active or a placebo HEPA air cleaner in the bedroom for 3 months. The active cleaner lowered cat allergen in the air. It did not change chest symptoms, peak flow, or rescue medicine use.

If you are allergic and keep the pet, the guideline says to consider these steps in order:

  1. Keep the pet outdoors, if that is safe and possible.
  2. If not, keep the pet out of the rooms you use most. The bedroom comes first.

NIEHS lists more steps to use together with those:

  • Put allergen-proof covers on mattresses and pillows.
  • Remove or replace carpets.
  • Vacuum often with a HEPA-filter vacuum cleaner, when the allergic person is not in the room.
  • Use a portable HEPA air cleaner in the allergic person's bedroom. Avoid ionic air cleaners that make ozone.
  • Bathe dogs. NIEHS says this "may decrease allergens if done at least twice a week."

These steps lower exposure. They do not replace your asthma medicine. If your symptoms are worst in bed, read why asthma is worse at night.

Is a "hypoallergenic" breed safer for asthma?

The studies do not support the claim. A 2012 Dutch study measured the main dog allergen, Can f 1, on dogs and in their homes. Breeds sold as hypoallergenic (Labradoodle, Poodle, Spanish Waterdog, and Airedale terrier) had higher allergen levels in hair and coat samples than the control breeds. Airborne allergen levels in the homes did not differ between breeds.

A 2011 study from a Detroit birth cohort compared house dust from homes with one dog. It tried four different ways to classify breeds as hypoallergenic. None showed a lower allergen level in the homes with "hypoallergenic" dogs.

Differences between breeds were small next to the differences between individual dogs of one breed. A breed label does not tell you how you will react to one animal.

How long does allergen stay after a pet leaves the home?

Months. A Johns Hopkins study collected dust from 15 homes for 9 to 43 weeks after the cat left. Allergen levels fell slowly in most homes. By 20 to 24 weeks, 8 of the 15 homes had reached the levels of homes with no cat. In 3 homes the levels were still high at 20 weeks or more.

So a two-week trial with the pet at a friend's house does not answer the question. Your home still holds the allergen.

You also cannot reach zero exposure. In a national survey of 831 US homes, a dog or cat had lived in 49.1% of the homes in the previous 6 months. Dog allergen was found in 100% of the homes and cat allergen in 99.9%. The allergens travel on clothing. Levels in homes with an indoor pet were far higher, so removal still cuts your exposure by a large amount.

Does removal help asthma? In a small Japanese study of 20 adults with new pet-allergic asthma, 10 gave up their pets and 10 kept them. After a year or more, airway sensitivity improved more in the removal group. None of that group needed an inhaled corticosteroid, and 9 of the 10 who kept their pets did. The study was not randomized, and the NIH panel judged it inconclusive.

What treatment options are there if you keep the pet?

The base is good asthma control with your usual medicine. See our guide to rescue and controller inhalers.

Allergy shots are a second option. The medical name is subcutaneous immunotherapy (SCIT). The NIH panel conditionally recommends SCIT as an add-on to standard medicine for people 5 years and older with mild to moderate allergic asthma. Asthma must be controlled when the shots start and through the course. The panel names pet allergy as one example of a suitable trigger. It describes the likely benefit as "possible small improvements" in symptoms and medicine use.

The clinician's guide gives these safety rules:

  • You get the shots in a clinic and stay for at least 30 minutes because of the risk of a body-wide allergic reaction.
  • You do not get a dose while you have asthma symptoms.
  • Shots are not for people with severe asthma.
  • Delayed reactions, more than 30 minutes after the injection, occur in about 15 percent of people.

The panel conditionally recommends against under-the-tongue immunotherapy (SLIT) as an asthma treatment.

Does a pet in infancy protect a child from asthma?

For allergy tests, possibly. For asthma, the best data say no effect in both directions. A Detroit birth cohort in JAMA followed 474 children to age 6 to 7. A positive skin test to any common allergen was found in 33.6% of children with no dog or cat in the first year of life, 34.3% with one, and 15.4% with two or more.

A pooled analysis of 11 European birth cohorts with over 22,000 children looked at asthma itself. It found no association between pets in the first 2 years of life and asthma at ages 6 to 10. The authors concluded that clinicians should not advise families to avoid pets, or to get pets, to prevent asthma.

These studies do not apply to a child who is already allergic to the family pet.

The bottom line

Most people with asthma do not need to give up a pet by default. Get tested first. If you are allergic and your asthma is hard to control, removal is the most direct option, and cat allergen can take 5 months or longer to clear from the home. If you keep the pet, combine several exposure measures, keep your asthma medicine on track, and ask an allergist about allergy shots. Do not rely on a "hypoallergenic" breed label.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are cats worse than dogs for asthma?

It depends on which animal you are allergic to. An allergy test can check cat and dog separately. In a national survey of US homes, average cat allergen levels in homes with an indoor cat were higher than dog allergen levels in homes with an indoor dog.

Will an air purifier let me keep my cat?

Not as the only measure. In a placebo-controlled trial, a bedroom HEPA air cleaner lowered airborne cat allergen but did not improve asthma symptoms, peak flow, or rescue medicine use. The NIH guideline advises a combination of measures because one measure alone often does not work.

Can I be allergic to pets if I do not own one?

Yes. Pet allergens travel on clothing. A national survey found dog allergen in 100% and cat allergen in 99.9% of US homes, and only about half of those homes had a dog or cat.

Do short-haired or hairless pets cause fewer asthma symptoms?

There is no good evidence for that. The allergens come from skin, saliva, and urine, and fur only carries them. Studies of dog breeds found no lower allergen level in homes with so-called hypoallergenic dogs.

Do allergy shots work for pet allergy and asthma?

They can help some people. The NIH expert panel conditionally recommends allergy shots as an add-on for people 5 and older with mild to moderate allergic asthma that is controlled. The expected benefit is small, and the shots carry a risk of allergic reactions, so they are given in a clinic.
