How Do You Know When Your Inhaler Is Empty?

September 22, 2026 7 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: Read the dose counter, not the spray. An inhaler is empty for practical purposes when its counter reaches 0, or when it passes the discard date on its label, whichever comes first. Aerosol inhalers keep spraying after the labeled number of doses, but the ProAir HFA label says the amount of medicine in each spray cannot be assured after that point. If your inhaler has no counter, the only reliable method is to write down each puff and subtract from the labeled total.

Why can an inhaler still spray after it is empty?

A metered-dose inhaler is overfilled on purpose. The FDA explained why in its March 2003 guidance for industry on dose counters. Extra formulation is needed to keep each spray consistent through the labeled number of doses.

The guidance gives an example. An inhaler labeled for 120 actuations may expel 20 to 30 more. The drug in those extra sprays may be inconsistent, and with continued use it becomes negligible. Inactive ingredients can make up 95 to 99 percent of the formulation. So an inhaler used past its label count may look like it delivers a full spray when it does not.

This is the tail-off problem. The Symbicort label says it plainly: your inhaler may not feel empty and may continue to operate, but you will not get the right amount of medicine if you keep using it.

For a rescue inhaler, the risk is real. In a 2003 telephone survey of 500 U.S. families with asthma, 87 of 342 bronchodilator users (25%) said they had found their inhaler empty during an asthma episode. Seven of them had to call 911. Of those 87, 82% judged the inhaler empty only when absolutely nothing came out (Annals of Allergy, Asthma and Immunology).

How do dose counters on aerosol inhalers work?

The 2003 FDA guidance recommended that makers of new metered-dose inhalers build in a dose counter. It set out a few design goals:

  • The counter should show clearly when the inhaler is near the end of its labeled doses, and when it has reached that number.
  • A numeric counter should count down to zero, not up.
  • If some error cannot be avoided, the counter should not undercount. An undercount could make you think medicine is left when it is not.

The guidance also covered lock-out devices that stop the inhaler after the last dose. It said a lock-out is not recommended for rescue bronchodilators, because even a partial dose could be life saving. That is why your albuterol inhaler still sprays at 0. The counter, not the canister, tells you when to stop relying on it.

What do the ProAir, Ventolin, Flovent and Symbicort labels say?

Each label describes its own counter. The numbers below come from the current FDA labeling for each product.

  • ProAir HFA. The label describes a 200-actuation canister. A black dot shows in the window until the inhaler is primed with 3 sprays, then the counter reads 200. It counts in units of 2, so an arrow between 190 and 188 means 189 sprays are left. At 20 the numbers turn red. At 0 the background turns solid red. Throw it away at 0 or after the expiration date, whichever comes first.
  • Ventolin HFA. The label lists 60 or 200 inhalations. The counter starts at 064 or 204, because priming uses the first sprays. It counts down by 1 and stops at 000. At 020, refill your prescription. Throw it away at 000 or 12 months after you opened the foil pouch, whichever comes first.
  • Flovent HFA. The label lists 120 inhalations. The counter starts at 124 and stops at 000. Refill at 020. At 000, throw it away, because you may not receive the right amount of medicine.
  • Symbicort. The label lists 60 or 120 inhalations. After priming, the counter reads 60 or 120. As the arrow nears 20, a yellow area appears as a refill reminder. Throw it away at 0 or 3 months after you take it out of its foil pouch, whichever comes first.

Two points stand out. First, the discard date can come before 0. A Symbicort inhaler used only now and then can still show doses after 3 months, and the label still says to throw it away. Second, the counters cannot be reset. The ProAir and Ventolin labels both tell you never to change the numbers or take the counter apart. For how many puffs a day the albuterol labels allow, see how often you can use an albuterol inhaler.

Why is the float test not recommended?

The float test means dropping the metal canister into a bowl of water and judging how full it is by how it floats. The labels now forbid it.

The ProAir HFA label says never to immerse the canister in water to determine how full it is. The Symbicort label uses almost the same words. The 2003 FDA guidance gave two reasons. Testing methods such as shaking or floating the canister are unreliable, and the float test may damage the inhaler.

Neither method can tell you how many full doses are left.

How do dry powder inhalers show they are empty?

Dry powder inhalers do not spray, so tail-off works differently. Each dose is a separate blister or measured portion of powder. When the blisters are used up, there is nothing left to inhale. The FDA guidance noted that multidose dry powder inhalers already had dose counters built in.

  • Advair Diskus. The label describes a strip of 60 blisters. After 55 doses, the numbers 5 to 0 show in red. Throw it away 1 month after you open the foil pouch or when the counter reads 0, whichever comes first.
  • Breo Ellipta. The label lists 30 doses (14 in a sample or institutional pack). When fewer than 10 doses remain, the left half of the counter shows red. Throw it away 6 weeks after you open the tray or at 0, whichever comes first.

The Breo Ellipta instructions add a useful check. The counter should drop by 1 when you slide the cover open and hear the click. If it does not count down, the inhaler will not deliver the medicine, and the label says to call your healthcare provider or pharmacist. What if your inhaler has no counter?

The 2003 guidance was aimed at inhalers in development, not ones already on the market. If yours has no counter, the FDA guidance named only one reliable method: track each actuation in writing and subtract the total from the labeled number.

  1. Find the labeled number. It is printed on the box and in the label, for example 60, 120 or 200 inhalations.
  2. Count priming sprays. Priming sprays come out of the same total. Ventolin HFA, for example, uses 4 priming sprays, which is why its counter starts at 204.
  3. Keep a written tally. A strip of tape on the box works. Mark every puff, including extra puffs during symptoms.
  4. Estimate the run-out date for daily inhalers. Divide the labeled number by the puffs you use each day. A 120-puff canister used 4 puffs a day lasts 30 days.

A rescue inhaler is harder to track because use is irregular. The survey above found that only 36% of bronchodilator users had ever been told to keep track of doses (PubMed). If you are going through rescue canisters faster than expected, that is worth tracking as a symptom in itself. Your asthma action plan should say what to do when rescue use rises.

The bottom line

You cannot tell an empty inhaler by the spray, a shake or a bowl of water. Use the counter and the discard date on your label. ProAir HFA turns red at 20 and solid red at 0. Ventolin HFA and Flovent HFA say to refill at 020. Symbicort shows yellow near 20. If your inhaler has no counter, keep a written tally and replace the canister at its labeled number.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use the extra puffs after my inhaler counter reaches zero?

The labels say not to rely on them. ProAir HFA says the labeled amount of medicine cannot be assured after 0, even though the canister will keep working. If 0 arrives during an attack, follow your action plan and get a new inhaler as soon as you can.

Why did my Ventolin counter start at 204 instead of 200?

The Ventolin HFA counter starts at 204 or 064 depending on canister size. The extra numbers cover the priming sprays you release into the air before first use. After priming, the count matches the 200 or 60 labeled inhalations.

My Symbicort still shows doses left after 3 months. Should I keep it?

The Symbicort label says to throw it away at 0 or 3 months after you take it out of the foil pouch, whichever comes first. Writing the pouch-opening date on the box makes this easy to track. Ask your pharmacist if you are unsure about the date.

Does a lighter inhaler mean it is almost empty?

Not reliably. The FDA's 2003 guidance says inactive ingredients can make up 95 to 99 percent of the formulation, and the canister is overfilled by design. Weight, sound and shaking do not tell you how many full doses remain.

What should I do if my counter seems stuck?

The Breo Ellipta label says that if the counter does not count down when you hear the click, the inhaler will not deliver medicine, and you should call your healthcare provider or pharmacist. For any inhaler, do not try to fix or reset the counter yourself. Take it to your pharmacy.
